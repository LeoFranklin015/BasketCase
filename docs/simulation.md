# Simulation: 60 days, 4 scenarios, 20 seeds each

Generated by `cargo run -p sim --release`, which also writes `web/public/sim.json` (the data behind the `/sim` page). Do not edit this file by hand: `cargo run -p sim --release -- --check` fails when it differs from a fresh run.

## What is simulated

One coin, from its fair-launch window to day 60, in hourly steps (1440 steps). Each scenario runs with 20 seeds; every number below is the median across seeds unless it says otherwise (`sim.json` also has p10 and p90 for every metric and day). The mechanism is not re-implemented: every buy, sell, burn, graduation, pool trade and redeem calls the same `curve-math` functions as the coin contract, on the same raw 1e18-scaled integers. The one piece of contract arithmetic outside `curve-math`, the fee on a buy capped at graduation, mirrors `contracts/coin/src/trade.rs`. After every action the simulator checks that the BU the coin owes (reserve + surplus + owed fees + pool) never exceeds the BU its vault holds; a violation aborts the run.

Mechanism parameters (from `constants.json`):

- Curve: 5,000 virtual BU, 1,073,000,000 virtual tokens, graduation at 20,000 BU of reserve (858.4M tokens sold).
- Trade fee 1%, split creator 50% / vault 30% / protocol 20%. The vault share goes to the surplus before graduation and to the reserve after.
- Graduation puts 20% of the backing in the pool; redeems pay `reserve / supply` less 0.5%, which stays in the reserve.
- A challenge must burn at least 3% of its slot's value and beat the defenders; epochs are 7 days.

**Floor.** Before graduation the floor is `P0 + s` (the curve's lowest price plus the surplus per token): no sell pays less per token, before the fee. After graduation it is `reserve / supply`, where supply includes the pool's tokens. **Price** is the price of the next token: the curve price plus `s`, or the pool's spot price after graduation.

## Key results (medians across seeds)

- **Steady growth:** supply grows from 444.7M to 733.6M tokens as buyers arrive. Fees alone lift the floor from 4.68 to 4.94 BU per 1M tokens (+5%), while the price climbs from 13.62 to 46.87, so the floor ends at 10.6% of the price.
- **Pump and dump:** the price peaks at 65.77 on day 4 and is 20.20 by day 60 (-69%); circulating supply drops from a peak of 787.1M to 552.1M as holders sell back to the curve. The floor never falls (4.69 to 5.10): sells pay out the curve and their own share of the surplus, never other holders' share.
- **Challenge war:** with the same buyers as the steady scenario, weekly challenge and defend burns remove 47.9M tokens (6.7% of circulating supply) by day 60. The day-60 floor is 6.92 against 4.94 without burns (+40%), and 15.8% of the price against 10.6%. That is after 3 winning challenges per run, each costing the vault a 1% to 2% solver cut on the seat.
- **The challenge minimum binds.** A winning challenge must burn at least 3% of the seat's value, which here is 0.62% of circulating supply (p10 0.56%, p90 0.79%). Challengers aiming just past it, plus defenders answering, burn 0.99% of supply in a median epoch (p10 0.79%), so the 1% weekly cap this scenario sets is reached in most epochs. A challenge burning less than 0.56% of supply would have failed in at least 90% of these epochs.
- **Graduation:** 20 of 20 runs graduate, on day 7 (median). The floor jumps from `P0 + s` to `reserve / supply`: 18.34 on day 8, the first day every run has graduated. When demand fades the pool price falls toward the floor (on day 14 the floor is 99.4% of the price), sells start paying out by redeeming because the matching path pays whichever is larger, and redeems burn 245.0M tokens by day 60 while the floor rises to 18.78. Holders bought high in the run-up: on day 14 the average cost across all holders is 79.82, so the floor covers 23% of what the average holder paid.

## Assumptions

Every behaviour is a small rule with a fixed number, so each result can be traced back. The numbers are also in `sim.json` under `params` and each scenario's `agents`.

**Time and randomness**
- Hourly steps for 60 days. Four seeded ChaCha8 streams per seed: arrivals (who arrives when, ticket sizes, each holder's habits), decisions (daily sell rolls), rituals, and the basket. Seed *n* gets the same basket path in every scenario, and the steady and challenge-war scenarios get the same buyers, so their difference is the rituals.
- All model arithmetic is integer; the files are byte-identical on any machine.

**Value of a BU**
- 1 BU = $1 at launch (`launch.bu_usd_default` in `constants.json`; a real coin picks its own at launch), so graduation (20,000 BU of reserve) means about $20,000 raised. Agents act in BU, so this value only scales the USD columns.
- The basket's USD value follows a random walk with 3% daily volatility and no drift (an integer approximation of a normal draw). It moves only the USD columns in `sim.json`; agents trade in BU and do not react to it.

**Agents**
- Fair-launch window: every launch deposit pays its fee, the nets are pooled into one curve buy, and the tokens are split pro rata.
- Retail buyers arrive at random (60 one-minute slots per hour, each a buyer with probability rate / 1,440). Ticket sizes in BU: 5 (10%), 10 (20%), 25 (25%), 50 (20%), 100 (15%), 250 (8%), 500 (2%); mean 63.75.
- Each retail holder checks once a day at its own hour and, in this order: churns out (sells everything) with the scenario's daily chance; or, if the price is at its take-profit multiple of its average cost (150%, 200%, 300%, 500%, drawn per holder), sells half with probability 50%; or, if the price is the scenario's panic threshold below its cost, panic-sells everything with the scenario's daily chance.
- Holders compare the next-token price with their average cost, fees included (average-cost method).
- Early holders (steady, challenge war) join the fair launch with 250 BU each and never sell.
- Whales (pump and dump) join the fair launch with 800 to 1,500 BU each and sell everything in 4 hourly chunks, starting at a random hour between day 4 and day 7.
- Fees owed to the creator and the protocol are claimed at the end of each day.

**Rituals (challenge war)**
- The basket is one anchored asset and one 50% seat; challengers always target the seat.
- Each 7-day epoch the challengers aim for 100% to 130% of the challenge minimum as it stands at the epoch's start. They turn that into tokens at the current burn value, clamp it to 0.1% to 0.8% of circulating supply, and burn it in 2 or 3 equal parts at random hours (none in the epoch's last 18 hours).
- Defenders answer each challenge burn with probability 50%, 1 to 12 hours later, burning 50% to 130% of its size.
- All burns in an epoch are capped at 1% of supply at the epoch's start, first come first served: once the cap is reached, later burns that epoch, from either side, are cut. The biggest bag on each side burns, out of the early holders' bags.
- At the epoch's close the challenge wins if its tally beats the defense and is at least the challenge minimum (3% of the seat's share of the vault). A win costs the vault the solver's cut: 1% to 2% of the seat's value, drawn per swap.
- Where that loss lands: before graduation it comes out of the surplus, not the reserve. The reserve is the curve's own position, and cutting it while `sold` stays the same breaks `x * y >= k`: `Curve::sell` would underflow on a small sell or hand the whole cut to one seller. Taking it all from the surplus is the cautious choice. It lowers `s`, and so the floor, more than a cut spread pro rata over reserve and surplus would. After graduation the loss is shared by the reserve and the pool pro rata.
- 8 epochs close in 60 days; the next one starts and is cut off at day 60 before it closes.

**After graduation**
- Buys go through the pool. A sell pays the larger of the pool's output after the fee and the redeem value after the 0.5% redeem fee.
- One agent the brief did not list: an arbitrageur. Every hour, while buying 1% of the pool's BU and redeeming the tokens pays more than it costs, it does so (up to 50 times an hour). It stands for anyone who would take that free money, and it keeps the pool price from staying below the floor. It trades rarely (see the per-run counts above).

## Caveats

- **Behaviour is assumed, not fitted.** Arrival rates, ticket sizes and selling rules are simple guesses, set so each scenario tells its story; no rule was calibrated against real launchpad data. Treat the levels as illustrations and the comparisons between scenarios as the result.
- **The basket is one number.** The BU's USD value is a single random walk; the agents never react to it, and there is no basket crash scenario here.
- **No adversarial trading beyond the arbitrageur.** No sandwiching, no sniping (the fair-launch window removes the first-block edge), no wash trading to farm the creator fee.
- **The swap is a fixed haircut.** A winning challenge is modelled as a 1% to 2% BU loss, not as a Dutch auction with real bidders; the replacement asset's later performance is not modelled.
- **The burn cap shapes the war.** The challenge minimum (3% of the seat's value) costs more than half of the 1% weekly cap in tokens, so the cap is reached in most epochs, and its first-come order partly decides who wins a contested week. A larger cap means more burning and a higher floor; a smaller one means no swaps at all.
- **Factions are tiny.** Three challengers and three defenders burning from bags they got at launch.
- **The floor before graduation is conservative.** `P0 + s` is what the last seller is guaranteed; the average backing per token, `(reserve + surplus) / circulating`, is higher.
- **Percentile bands are per metric.** The median price and the median floor on a day can come from different seeds.

## `sim.json` format

`{ generated_by, params, scenarios: [{ id, title, description, agents, stats, days }] }`. Each entry in `days` is `{ day, supply, circulating, price_bu, floor_bu, surplus_per_token, vault_bu, burned_total, redeemed_total, holders_avg_cost_bu, floor_pct_of_price, bu_usd, price_usd, floor_usd, mode, graduated_share }`. Every metric except `day`, `mode` and `graduated_share` is `{ p10, median, p90 }` across seeds. Amounts are whole tokens or whole BU (not raw 1e18 units); prices are BU (or USD) per token; `floor_pct_of_price` is a percentage. `mode` is the mode most seeds are in that day, and `graduated_share` the fraction of seeds in Index mode. Day 0 is the moment the fair-launch window settles; day *d* is the end of the *d*-th day.
